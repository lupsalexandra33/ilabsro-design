    The hypothesis

    Think first. Then let AI challenge it.

    Founders who think first and use AI as a challenger produce better, more defensible work and own more of their reasoning than those who let AI write version one.

    A reasoned bet, not a proof. The evidence is recent and partly correlational. The Bootcamp 1 to Bootcamp 2 deliverable-quality measures and the AI-use log are how we find out.

      What we're seeing in the generation

      This generation didn't get the device discipline that older builders or the next generation of schoolchildren are getting. The program can work around that gap or be one of the few places that closes it.

      The cohort, mostly mid-20s, were teenagers during the shift to phone-based, offload-everything defaults. What shows up in pitches and interviews is fluent, competent-sounding output with less underneath it, and less ownership of the thinking when pushed live.

      On the evidence. Gerlich (2025) found younger participants showed higher AI dependence and lower critical-thinking scores than older ones in the same study. It is a correlational finding, and the revamp treats it as part of a bet. See the evidence.

      Hub / Evidence
      Research and evidence base

      A bet grounded in evidence. Not a proof.

      Ordered by how directly it bears on our founders, adults in their mid-20s. We keep two things apart: how robust a finding is, and how well it transfers to what we do.

      A · The problem

      Founders are offloading the thinking step.

      Not only the writing. Interviews, competitive analysis and the pitch narrative go to AI, producing more polished output with less of the founder's own reasoning underneath.

      MIT Media Lab, Your Brain on ChatGPT (2025)

      Connectivity scaled down as external support went up. ChatGPT-group essays clustered together, statistically homogeneous within each topic. The authors named the accumulated effect cognitive debt.

      We had access to the preprinted, under-review version of the study.

      Mixed agesCorrelational

      Gerlich, cognitive offloading and critical thinking (2025)

      A significant negative correlation between frequent AI-tool use and critical-thinking ability, mediated by cognitive offloading. Younger participants showed higher dependence and lower critical-thinking scores than older ones.

      MechanismOriginal finding, 2011

      The Google effect

      People forget information they are confident they can look up again, and remember where something is better than the thing itself. The plausible extension, not yet demonstrated, is that AI chat tools push this from facts to reasoning.

      Used as a mechanism, not as a recently reinforced finding.

      Practitioner literatureTeachable skill

      Why some founders' AI output is worse

      Prompt wording matters far less than the quality and specificity of the material and domain knowledge fed in. Output tracks the evidence, correction and iteration the founder brings, not the model. Feeding AI real source material and interrogating its output is context engineering, which is what we want to teach.

      Practitioner evidence, not peer-reviewed research [5][6].

      The core of the case

      AI offloading has a measurable cost, it lands harder on younger users, and the antidote is not abstinence but doing your own thinking first and using the model as a challenger.

      B · Why this cohort

      The risk concentrates in the founders we serve.

      Our founders were teenagers during the shift to phone-based, offload-everything defaults. This context does not prove our interventions work. It explains why this group is exposed.

      Narrative book

      Haidt, The Anxious Generation (2024)

      Four harms (social deprivation, sleep disruption, attention fragmentation, addiction) track the smartphone era and concentrate in people who were adolescents during the transition. A threshold cited in Congressional testimony: 1 to 2 hours a day of social media shows no measurable harm, 3 to 4 hours does.

      A strong narrative rather than proof. Useful for framing, not as a causal claim.

      Policy reversal

      Sweden's reversal

      After about two decades of digitising classrooms, Swedish fourth-grade reading proficiency declined between the 2016 and 2021 assessments. In 2023 the government funded printed textbooks (about 685 million kronor) and moved to keep devices largely away from under-sixes.

      Not a controlled experiment. The causal link is the government's read and is contested. It signals institutional direction.

      Children, 3 to 11Extrapolated

      Screen time and attention

      A longitudinal study of just over a thousand children linked screen use to weaker sustained attention and reading to stronger attention, with print reading tied to better comprehension than digital.

      We extrapolate that the same mechanism applies to people in their mid-20s.

      Counterweight

      School phone bans

      Positive results exist, but a review of about two dozen studies in a dozen countries judged the evidence weak and inconclusive, and found a short-term spike in disciplinary incidents in year one.

      The case for device discipline is real but not one-sided. Saying so keeps the rest credible.

      C · The countermeasure

      Force original thinking before any model is opened.

      Read this research as mechanism and precedent: why paper-first and dialogic methods plausibly work, and where they already work in practice. Not as proof that they improve startup outcomes.

      StudentsMixed replication

      Handwriting versus typing

      Across several studies, laptop note-takers did worse on conceptual questions than longhand note-takers, apparently because laptops invite verbatim transcription while handwriting forces paraphrase and synthesis. The closest analog evidence to our setting.

      A 2019 replication and later work found the effect does not hold consistently. We treat the mechanism as plausible, not settled.

      RobustYoung children

      Embodied cognition

      In pre-literate children, the brain's reading circuit activates during letter perception only after handwriting practice, not after typing or tracing.

      Cited as a mechanism for why self-produced work is encoded differently, not as evidence about founders.

      Design inspiration

      Montessori, Waldorf, Reggio Emilia

      Concrete before abstract; authoring your own lesson books; the environment and visible documentation as a third teacher that makes a group's thinking legible in real time.

      Design inspiration and mechanism, not outcome evidence for adults. Keep that distinction explicit wherever this is presented.

      Adult professional method

      Design Sprint and Socratic seminar

      The GV Design Sprint starts with individual paper sketching (Crazy 8s) before any group or digital work, and is already named in our Module 2 deck. The Socratic seminar (live, unscripted, no notes, no slides) maps directly onto pitch Q&A and mock-interview debriefs.

      D · Limits

      What this evidence does not show.

      None of it proves that paper-first exercises make founders build better companies. The strongest findings are about children and transfer weakly. The most relevant findings, on adults and AI, are recent and partly correlational. The chain from "handwriting aids encoding" to "better pitch, better product" is unvalidated.

      That is the reason to state the approach correctly: analog-first with AI as a challenger is a reasoned bet, grounded in a plausible cognitive mechanism, an adult professional precedent already in our curriculum, and our own observation of this cohort's fluent-but-shallow output.

      How we settle it. On our own teams: the share of teams whose deliverable quality improves between Bootcamp 1 and Bootcamp 2, and the AI-use log.

      E · The hypothesis we test

      Three things we can act on.

      1. The analog-first sequenceOffloading has a measurable cost and lands hardest on mid-20s founders. The countermeasure puts "build it yourself first" into specific existing blocks.
      2. Challenger skills and How AI Tricks YouTurn "use AI as an adversary, not an author" into something teachable.
      3. The AI-risk registerNames the failure modes this design exists to prevent: fabricated research, fluency bias, idea homogenization.

      The same logic runs through the parts that aren't about AI. Feedback is where thinking gets stress-tested, so the mentor redesign, the coaching response to low coachability and the smaller cohort all aim at fewer, better-supported teams who can absorb hard feedback rather than deflect it to a model.

      Stated plainly

      Founders who think first and use AI as a challenger produce better, more defensible work and own more of their reasoning than those who let AI write version one.

      The Bootcamp 1 to Bootcamp 2 measures are how we find out.

        Analog pedagogy

        Four stages, in this order.

        Founders are producing more content than ever, but less of it reflects their own thinking. The goal is not to reject AI. It is to make sure founders build their own thinking before AI amplifies it.

        1. Think in analogWrite, sketch, colour-code and map assumptions on paper, before any external help.
        2. Think togetherPresent unfinished work to peers, take real questions, defend the reasoning out loud.
        3. Challenge with AIOnly after an original version exists. AI challenges assumptions, finds blind spots, plays devil's advocate.
        4. Human synthesisThe founder makes the final call. The output stays human-authored.

        The workflow by the end of the program

        Observe→Think→Sketch→Discuss→Build→Challenge with AI→Synthesize→Decide
        Prompt→Copy→Present
        AI is

        A challenger, a critic, a devil's advocate, a reviewer, a stress tester, a debate partner, a perspective generator.

        AI is not

        The founder, the product manager, the storyteller, the strategist.

        Worked example: building the pitch

        Today, many founders ask AI to write the pitch first. The new sequence:

        1. Write the pitch yourself.
        2. Present it.
        3. Get feedback from other founders.
        4. Improve it yourself.
        5. Use AI to critique it.
        6. Let AI challenge the narrative, find the weaknesses, ask the hard questions.
        7. Rewrite the pitch yourself.

        The hidden curriculum

        Founders whose thinking gets stronger because of AI, rather than weaker through dependence on it.

        By the end, founders should trust their own intuition, reason independently, tolerate uncertainty, build ideas from first principles, defend their arguments, improve through critique rather than generation, and use AI intentionally instead of reflexively.

        Challenger skills

        Teach AI as an adversary, not a co-author.

        The same four-stage sequence, applied: build it on paper, discuss with peers, pressure-test it against an AI challenger, then bring both to a mentor for the final synthesis.

        mom-test-interrogator

        Flags Mom Test violations in interview questions or transcripts: leading questions, would-you-buy-this hypotheticals, praise-fishing.

        Is this question leading the witness?Did I praise-fish instead of getting a real answer?
        red-team-my-pitch

        Runs a pitch script against the existing red-flags list, as a first pass before a mentor sees it.

        Where does this pitch go vague on value?Am I hiding behind buzzwords?

        Has the most source material and is likely the fastest first build.

        blind-spot-competitor-hunt

        Searches for competitors a team didn't list, especially indirect and ecosystem ones.

        Who am I actually competing with?What's the do-nothing threat I'm missing?
        assumption-stress-test

        Separates falsifiable, tested hypotheses from vague padding, the kind the "10+ hypotheses" requirement invites.

        Which of these are restated opinions?Is this testable as written?
        juror-simulator

        Generates hard live follow-up questions from gaps in a pitch, for solo rehearsal or for mentors preparing Bootcamp 2.

        What's a juror going to hit me with?What exposes the weakest part?
        How mentors plug in

        Mentors co-author the rubric each skill enforces. One shared, mentor-authored standard is the actual fix for contradictory advice. Sign-up forms get a field for AI and tooling competency, used for routing, not as a requirement.

        Risk, named up front. Founders can learn to satisfy the rubric mechanically instead of reflecting. Mitigate by keeping output as a discussion prompt for the mentor session, not a pass or fail gate, and by varying prompts.

        Proposed course

        How AI tricks you.

        A standalone workshop or a new block inside Module 1, built in the same format as the existing decks.

        1. How AI flatters youFluency bias: polished output reads as more competent than it is. Most pitch red flags are what AI-flattened writing looks like.
        2. What you lose when you offloadCognitive debt, the critical-thinking correlation and its age skew, the Google effect extended from facts to reasoning.
        3. How AI fails silently, hereFabricated competitors and synthetic interviews, idea homogenization, AI as an invisible third mentor.
        4. Context engineering, the actual skillContext poisoning, distraction, confusion and clash. Context clash is the mentor-contradiction problem applied to a machine. The Generator, Reflector, Curator loop: build, critique, refine.
        5. Practical exerciseFounders red-team their own real deliverable live with the challenger skills, mentor-supervised. Output is a discussion prompt, not a grade.

        Deliverable: an AI-use log per team, a plain statement of what was AI-assisted versus self-generated, which also operationalises the integrity rule.

        AI risks worth watching

        Five ways AI fails quietly.

        Risk

        Fabricated research

        AI will produce a plausible competitor list or a synthetic interview that never happened. Needs a blunt integrity rule: transcripts traceable to a real person, competitor claims sourced.

        Risk

        Fluency bias

        AI-polished output reads as more competent than it is. Mentors and jurors risk scoring polish over substance.

        Risk

        Idea homogenization

        Similar models with similar framing push teams toward convergent business models and pitch language.

        Risk

        AI as a silent extra mentor

        A third, invisible voice added to the contradictory-advice problem, from a source nobody can see or moderate.

        Risk

        Data leakage and GDPR

        Interview transcripts contain personal data. Pasting them into consumer AI tools is a compliance problem for an EU-based program. Needs a plain data-handling rule.

        The problems

        Six reasons feedback turned into noise.

        Cohort sizing

        Cut after Bootcamp 2 plus one week.

        Of the 41 teams in 2026, the read is that only around 25 were worth the program's full resources. The current structure spreads mentor time across all of them equally.

        Why the buffer week

        Cutting at the closing bell of Bootcamp 2 judges teams before they act on the feedback they just got. Teams said one week between bootcamps wasn't enough, especially when feedback was contradictory and they needed a day or two to agree on a direction. The buffer means the cut measures whether a team applied feedback.

        Check before it becomes policy. The roughly 16 teams that didn't clear the bar are said to skew non-Bucharest, but there were only 15 non-Bucharest teams out of 41. Test the claim against per-team evaluation data and the travel-burden confound. The argument for a smaller cohort doesn't need it.

        The cut is not the only exit

        1. Checkpoint two weeks after the HackathonFor teams admitted on potential. If the startup still shows nothing, they drop or merge into another team.
        2. Ongoing deliverable evaluationLow quality two weeks running means out.
        3. At-risk shortlist after Bootcamp 2Expectations stated explicitly. The cut lands a week later.

        Same caveat as everywhere: both continuous mechanisms need separate B2C, B2B, software and hardware criteria, or the rubric that underserved hardware teams will also cut them.

        Makes private coaching feasible

        Splitting mentor and coaching hours across 25 teams instead of 41 is a real increase in attention per team.

        Needs real criteria, not vibes

        Bootcamp 1 already produces comparable artifacts per team: interview evidence, hypothesis rigor, competitor research depth.

        Mentor access isn't the gap

        Non-Bucharest teams get the same mentors. The known gap is travel burden; check whether it drags down their output.

        Go deeper · The EGV prize moves to IL Beyond
        Hub / IL Beyond
        IL Beyond · New track

        Replace the cliff with a slope.

        Reaching Demo Day was never the same as being fundable. IL Beyond takes the strongest teams from finalist to term sheet to money in the bank: Build, Sell, Raise.

        Why this exists

        Support stops exactly when teams need it.

        • Many teams graduate with a good demo and a real story but aren't investable yet: no incorporation, no cap table, no repeatable sales motion, sometimes no stable founding team.
        • Most stop building the week the program ends, when the accountability structure disappears.
        • Many don't know the IL team is still willing to help, so the relationship goes quiet by default.
        • Even when help is available, many don't ask, assuming the door is closed or that asking means admitting they're stuck.
        Objectives

        Measured in term sheets and money that lands.

        ≥2

        Companies finish investment-ready for a EUR 300k to 500k pre-seed round, meaning they sign a term sheet.

        ≥1

        Company reaches an actual investment process with EGV or another pre-seed institutional investor, meaning the money lands.

        The funnel, end to end

        Each decision about a team now carries forward instead of living in its own section.

        1. HackathonTeams cluster: any traction, high potential overall, high potential team (needs pivot), high potential idea (needs people).
        2. Core programBootcamps 1 and 2 generate real artifacts per team: interview evidence, hypothesis rigor, competitor research depth, deliverable ranking, analog outputs.
        3. The cutAfter Bootcamp 2 plus a week, evaluated against those artifacts.
        4. Demo Day or graduationOr, for alumni from earlier years, however far they got.
        5. IL BeyondOpen to this year's graduates and alumni from any past year. Selection reads the team's documentation rather than starting cold.
        6. S1 Build, S2 Sell, S3 RaiseNarrowing 6 to 3–4 to 2, ending in an actual investment.

        Team documentation. Every artifact across the funnel is one running record per team. Not a new system: a naming and ownership decision, so what already lives in Notion, Drive and coach notes is tagged consistently.

        Three stages

        Build. Sell. Raise.

        S1 · July to September · 6 teams

        Build

        PMF and a defined technical moat. The IL MVP becomes a product real external users interact with.

        S2 · October to December · 3–4 teams

        Sell

        Sales and investment readiness, including incorporation. One or two teams should exit with a term sheet.

        S3 · January to February · 2 teams

        Raise

        From term sheet to money in the bank, with angels and VCs brought in as co-investors.

        Stage 1: Build, in detailOutcome, deliverables, measures

        Outcome: active users, customers or design partners with real external usage; clear ICP and use case; major technical assumptions tested; an identified moat and credible roadmap; the core team intact and committed; clear evidence the team shipped materially over the summer.

        Losing a co-founder mid-stage is not automatically disqualifying. The program adds support, such as co-founder matching or conflict mediation, rather than a penalty.

        Deliverables Drafted, to confirm

        • Updated pitch deck reflecting PMF evidence.
        • A one-page technical roadmap naming the moat and the 100x scaling risk.
        • A log of active users or customers with evidence: usage data, a signed pilot, testimonials.
        • A written core-team commitment on roles and full-time involvement.

        What we measure: product milestones and real users; for software, iterations, integration and cybersecurity testing, benchmarks; for hardware, prototype maturity, reliability, BOM and COGS, manufacturing feasibility; execution velocity; B2B weekly users, activation and retention; B2C activation, D7 and D30 retention, repeat usage.

        Stage 2: Sell, in detailIncorporate and sign

        Outcome: incorporate the startup and sign commercial contracts while preparing for investment.

        Deliverables Drafted, to confirm

        • Incorporation documents, founder agreement, clean cap table.
        • At least one signed contract, LOI or purchase order.
        • A completed investor deck and a draft data room.
        • A financial model with key assumptions validated.

        Commercial evidence by type. B2B SaaS and DevTools: paying customers, paid pilots, recurring revenue. Enterprise and deep tech: paid PoC, signed pilot, design partnership with a procurement path. Consumer: transactions, retention, willingness to pay. Hardware: paid prototype or pilot, purchase order, credible manufacturing and unit economics.

        By November: a mature deck, a draft data room, a clear round size and use of funds tied to milestones.

        Stage 3: Raise, in detailTerm sheet to cash

        Outcome: investment signed, money in the bank. Reserved for teams already holding a term sheet.

        Angels and VCs need to put money in at this stage. The EUR 20,000 angel bridge is IL's own contribution to help a team cross from term sheet to cash, alongside third-party investment, not instead of it.

        • EGV leads or co-leads where relevant, with a defined process for bringing in co-investors.
        • Angels and pre-seed VCs invited by sector and check-size fit.
        • A 48-hour warm intro window after any pitch or investor meeting.
        • Angels get access before the round is fully priced or oversubscribed.
        • Teams pitch at existing investor meetups such as Tech Angels and Bravva rather than only an IL-owned room.

        Next step: validate this model directly with a handful of EGV's co-investors and independent angels in the Romanian ecosystem before building it out further.

        The EGV Investment Prize

        Moves from Demo Day to the end of Raise.

        Why. A EUR 500,000 ticket offered on Demo Day night goes to a team that is usually not incorporated, has no repeatable sales motion, and cannot yet absorb it. That mismatch produced the prize not being awarded on stage in 2026.

        What changes. EGV's capital is deployed at the end of Stage 3, into a team with a signed term sheet, alongside IL's own EUR 20,000 angel bridge. EGV stays embedded through mentors, speakers, jury, the monthly founder dinner and co-defining the investment-ready bar.

        What Demo Day keeps. The recognition awards: Grand Prize, Best Emerging Start-up, Best Product, Public Vote. Demo Day stays the celebration and the doorway into IL Beyond.

        Requires EGV's agreement to reposition the prize, and clear communication to teams that the path to EGV capital now runs through IL Beyond.

        Investment ready, defined

        Commercially, technically, organisationally.

        Commercially
        • Real users, design partners or customers.
        • Commercial validation fit to the business model.
        • A credible route to a scalable business, evidenced by a validated adoption channel or a small acquisition experiment.

        The scalability bar should be calibrated against how EGV and partner VCs judge it in practice; at pre-seed even VCs mostly eyeball it.

        Tech
        • A functioning product or technically validated prototype.
        • Credible technical differentiation.
        • A plan to scale: what breaks at 100x.
        Organisation
        • A stable, committed founding team.
        • An incorporated company with a clean equity structure.
        • IP owned by the company.
        • A clear use of the EUR 300k to 500k round.
        • An investment deck and a minimal data room.
        Selection for Stage 1

        Who gets in, and what they get.

        Not limited to the current graduating cohort. A team from 2023 or 2024 still building and showing real progress is a legitimate candidate.

        • At least 2 full-time founders.
        • At least 1 technical founder capable of building the core technology.
        • Meaningful know-how, data advantage, IP, engineering complexity or defensible architecture.
        • Venture-scale potential and a reason why 300k to 500k would accelerate the company.
        • Coachability and motivation, reassessed fresh at this stage rather than carried over from the core program.

        Process: a 1-hour interview plus a 1-hour coaching and feedback session with the core team and selected jury members, so selection gives something back. Homework and a follow-on interview are possible. Second phase: are founders shipping, selling, learning and deciding quickly; is traction getting stronger.

        What Innovation Labs provides
        • Direct team funding.
        • Accountability through periodic check-ups.
        • One dedicated coach plus help on demand.
        • Templates, an incorporation checklist, vetted providers.
        • Customer and investor introductions where possible.
        • Technical reviews.
        • Networking beyond the monthly founder dinner.
        Tracking cadence and the lead coachWeekly to monthly
        • Weekly: an email from founders: committed, happened, key KPI, biggest learning, biggest blocker, next commitment.
        • Every 2 weeks: a 30-minute check-in with the lead coach.
        • Monthly: a board-meeting-like 1-hour review, and a founder dinner with the core team, lead coaches, teams and possibly EGV.

        Verification, not just trust: load-bearing KPIs need evidence such as screenshots, analytics exports or signed documents.

        The lead coach follows the company for the whole accelerator and sits at a more senior level than a core-program coach, with real judgment on incorporation, cap tables and investor dynamics.

        Still open: whether the lead coach gets a percentage, for example an option inside the IL vehicle; owners for the tech and product reviews; whether IL pushes teams toward YC, Techstars or Seedcamp.

        Research

        How other programs handle this.

        Closest direct comparison

        Techstars [22]

        A three-month cohort ending in Demo Day, with a defined USD 220,000 investment and lifetime access to the mentor and alumni network. Alumni average a first post-program raise above USD 1M.

        Closest in stated purpose

        EIC Pre-Accelerator [23]

        Raises deep-tech readiness for larger EIC or national funding, with a lump-sum grant (EUR 300k to 500k in 2025, rising to EUR 500k to 1M in 2027) plus coaching and pitching bootcamps.

        Aimed at this exact gap

        Mountside Ventures [24]

        Free investor-readiness programmes for pre-seed and seed companies raising in 3 to 12 months: templates, investor updates, a data room checklist, deck guidance.

        Closest on size and coaching

        Alchemist Accelerator [25]

        Six months for B2B and enterprise startups, about USD 25,000 to 30,000 for about 5 percent, capped near 25 teams, every founder paired with an experienced coach.

        Go where investors already are

        Romania has few active pre-seed investors, so the constraint is reaching them at all. Syncing with Tech Angels and Bravva beats assembling a new room.

        The 48-hour window

        One demo-day playbook finds investor attention converts best within 48 hours of a pitch, with follow-up rates dropping sharply after.

        Early angel access

        The best access happens before a round is public. Warm intros and commitments before an event avoid a bidding war and signal confidence.
