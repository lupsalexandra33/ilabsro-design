Build for real problems.

A short challenge for students who would rather solve something than pitch something.

See what to submit How it works

The challenge

Find a real problem. Understand it. Show how you’d start.

We’re not asking for a startup, a prototype or a pitch. We’re asking for a clear problem, proof that you looked at it with your own eyes, and a first step you could take next week.

Who it’s for

  • Students from any programme, year or discipline.
  • Solo, or in a small team.
  • No startup idea or prototype needed. One honest conversation is a good start.

Eligibility is illustrative for this test page.

Example problems

Strong problems start with a person and a place.

Three problem spaces to show the level of detail we mean. They are illustrations, not briefs from real organisations, and you don’t have to pick one.

  1. A

    Who has it · Students and staff who can’t use stairs

    A step-free route through campus

    Route information is patchy or out of date, so people plan detours or skip the trip.

    Start by

    Walking one route with someone who uses it every day. Note where it breaks.

  2. B

    Who has it · Small canteens, cafés and people nearby

    Surplus food at closing time

    Good portions get binned at the end of the day, with no easy way to reach people who could use them.

    Start by

    Spending one closing shift in a kitchen. Count what leaves, and where it goes.

  3. C

    Who has it · Small volunteer groups and their coordinators

    Shifts that slip through the cracks

    Scheduling lives in chat threads and spreadsheets, so shifts get missed and the same few people cover.

    Start by

    Asking a coordinator to show last month’s schedule. Find out who covered the gaps.

The criteria

We’re looking for a clear problem, not a clever idea.

Four questions we’d ask of every brief. If you can answer yes to most of them, you’re in good shape.

  1. 01

    A real person

    Can you name who has the problem, and talk to them this week?

  2. 02

    Something you can observe

    Can you watch it happen, rather than imagine it?

  3. 03

    A real cost

    Does it cost someone time, money, trust or safety?

  4. 04

    A small first step

    Could you start with what you have, within a week?

One quick rewrite

Instead of

“An app that helps volunteers manage shifts.”

Starts with a solution.

Try

“Small volunteer groups lose shifts because scheduling is split across chat threads and a spreadsheet.”

Starts with a person, a place and what goes wrong.

How it works

Four steps. Start with a conversation.

  1. You start here 1

    Pick a problem

    Choose one you’ve seen first-hand or can get close to. Write it in a single sentence.

    Sample · week 1

  2. 2

    Talk to someone who has it

    Listen more than you explain. Notice what they do today, not what they say they’d use.

    Sample · weeks 1–2

  3. 3

    Write the brief

    One page: the problem, who has it, what you saw and your first step.

    Sample · week 3

  4. 4

    Submit and hear back

    Send it in. A person reads it properly and replies with feedback.

    Sample · week 4
