Nine stops, from the October pre-program to IL Beyond. Tap a stop to jump to it.

    What 2026 told us

    The program works. Teams told us where it breaks.

    Keep and protect

    • Networking and community are the main value teams take away.
    • Bootcamp 1 and the Pitch Prep Workshop are the highest-rated formats.
    • Pre-recorded theory worked: teams watched at their own pace and brought questions.
    • Outcomes after the program: investor inbound, ongoing mentor relationships.

    Fix in 2027

    • Hardware teams are underserved by a software-biased mentor pool.
    • Mentor advice contradicts itself, with no way to reconcile it.
    • Three weekends in a row for Hackathon, Bootcamp 1 and Bootcamp 2.
    • Deliverables uploaded, promised a review, never answered.
    • Hybrid and online formats lose the online audience and break continuity.
    • Travel burden falls on non-Bucharest teams.

    Source · 2026 cohort interviews. Coachability concerns come from direct observation, not transcripts.

    The pattern

    Polished output. Less thinking underneath.

    Founders are offloading the thinking step, not only the writing: the interviews, the competitive analysis, the pitch narrative.

    83%

    of ChatGPT users in the study could not quote a sentence from the essay they had just written.

    MIT Media Lab, 2025 · 54 participants · preprint under review
    666

    participants: frequent AI use correlated with lower critical thinking. Younger participants were more dependent.

    Gerlich, Societies, 2025 · correlational
    our teams

    Fluent, competent-sounding pitches and interviews, with less ownership of the reasoning when pushed live.

    Direct observation · 2026 cohort

    The research is recent and partly correlational. That is why the program does not rest on it alone and measures the effect on its own teams.

    The bet for 2027

    Think first. Use AI as a challenger.

    Not banned. Not a ghostwriter. AI enters once a founder has an original version, to argue with it.

    By the end of the program, a founder works like this

    Observe→Think→Sketch→Discuss→Build→Challenge with AI→Synthesize→Decide
    Prompt→Copy→Present
    Hypothesis · tested on our own teams in 2027

    The learning sequence

    Four stages, in this order.

    Applied inside blocks we already run, not added as new time.

    01

    Think in analog

    Write, sketch and map assumptions on paper before any assistance.

    02

    Think together

    Present unfinished work to peers and defend the reasoning out loud.

    03

    Challenge with AI

    Only now. AI tests assumptions, finds blind spots and argues back.

    04

    Human synthesis

    The founder makes the call. The output stays human-authored.

    AI is

    a challenger, a critic, a devil's advocate, a reviewer, a stress tester, a debate partner.

    AI is not

    the founder, the product manager, the storyteller, the strategist.

    The evidence

    A reasoned bet. Not a proof.

    Mechanism · plausible

    Writing by hand forces synthesis

    Longhand note-takers beat laptop note-takers on concepts, but a 2019 replication did not hold consistently. Embodied-cognition findings are robust, but about young children.

    Adult precedent · direct

    Already a professional method

    The GV Design Sprint starts with individual paper sketching (Crazy 8s), and it is already in our Module 2 deck. The Socratic seminar maps onto pitch Q&A.

    Context · not proof

    Why this cohort is exposed

    Haidt, Sweden's return to printed books and a screen-time study explain the generation. A review of school phone bans found that evidence weak and inconclusive.

    How we settle it

    The share of teams whose deliverable quality improves from Bootcamp 1 to Bootcamp 2, read alongside each team's AI-use log.

    Stop 3 · AI literacy and curation track

    Rank it. Source it. Then challenge it.

    Deliverable ranking

    Three anonymised 2026 deliverables, strong to weak. Teams rank them and justify.

    Competitive analysis exam

    Every data point needs a source. Unsourced research is invalid.

    Master prompt

    One per team: it challenges ideas, asks for sources and lists assumptions.

    Deck cross-review

    Teams answer questions about a pitch deck they did not write.

    The integrity line

    AI-fabricated interviews and competitors don't count. Real people and real sources, or the deliverable is invalid.

    Every submission adds the same AI-use log: what we planned, what we did, what surprised us, what we changed, what we asked AI, what we verified.

    Challenger skills

    Tools that argue back, not tools that write.

    mom-test-interrogator

    "Is this question leading the witness?"

    red-team-my-pitch

    "Where does this pitch go vague on value?"

    blind-spot-competitor-hunt

    "What's the do-nothing threat I'm missing?"

    assumption-stress-test

    "Is this testable, or unfalsifiable as written?"

    juror-simulator

    "What will a juror hit me with?"

    Mentors co-author the rubric

    One shared standard, checked the same way for every team. That is the fix for contradictory advice.

    Output is a prompt, not a grade

    Teams could learn to satisfy a rubric mechanically. Results feed the mentor session, never a pass or fail gate.

    Proposed for 2027

    • Hands-on mentors, 2–3 per team: one business, one tech.
    • PODS: 3–5 teams, a facilitator and 2–3 mentors, weekly for the whole program.
    • A vetted mentor core, admitted after a 30-minute conversation.
    • Hands-on mentors evaluate deliverables, not only advise.
    • A way to fix a bad pairing instead of enduring it.
    • Weekly coach checkpoint with a short form the core team can read.

    Stop 4 · Bootcamp 2

    Keep the brutal feedback. Add private coaching beside it.

    BeforePrepare and expect

    Office hours, online mentoring, Back to Fundamentals moved here. Expectations sent in advance.

    DuringAbsorb and pivot

    A working session to internalise feedback, with coaches. Pivot decision cards against the triggers list.

    AfterSay who is at risk

    A shortlist of at-risk teams, with expectations stated explicitly.

    Decision

    Group feedback stays public and unsoftened. Private 1:1 coaching runs next to it for the self-awareness work a room can't do.

    Coachability also becomes a selection criterion and a stated value. Private coaching is only feasible once the cohort drops to about 25 teams.

    What we decide next

    Ten calls to make it real.

    • Travel burden vs. the Hub downgrade: stipend, hybrid or clustered scheduling.
    • Cut criteria and the four rubrics: B2C, B2B, software, hardware.
    • Integrity rule for AI-assisted research and interviews.
    • Juror code of conduct and a safeguarding reporting channel.
    • Which analog exercises to pilot first, and where.
    • Challenger skills build order. red-team-my-pitch looks fastest.
    • Private coaching: who, how many hours, at about 25 teams.
    • An owner for the WhatsApp community.
    • A name for the analog initiative. Not "Unplugged".
    • Validate IL Beyond Stage 3 with angels and VCs.

    The hidden curriculum

    Founders whose thinking gets stronger because of AI.

    Not the ones who prompt fastest. The ones whose thinking is worth amplifying. Bootcamp 1 to Bootcamp 2 will tell us if the bet holds.
